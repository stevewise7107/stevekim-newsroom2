Government inaugurates task force to curb school unrest

24 November 2026 · Source: Kenya News Agency · Edited for this student publication

The government has inaugurated a multi-stakeholder task force mandated to investigate the causes of student unrest and recommend sustainable measures to improve safety, discipline and order in learning institutions.

The announcement places renewed attention on the conditions that shape student welfare, including communication between school leadership and learners, conflict resolution, mental and social support, and the handling of grievances. The task force’s recommendations will be important because lasting solutions require more than punishment after an incident.

Students, teachers, parents and education officials all have a role in building environments where concerns can be raised early and addressed fairly. The public should follow the task force’s findings and the government’s response.

Why it matters: Learning institutions are expected to protect students while supporting academic achievement. Policies will only make a difference when they are implemented consistently and learners are meaningfully heard.

Lamu: a living museum by the Indian Ocean

Travel feature · 5 min read

On Kenya’s northern coast, Lamu Old Town offers a close encounter with centuries of Swahili culture. Its narrow streets, coral-stone buildings, mangrove timber and elaborately carved wooden doors tell stories of trade, faith, craftsmanship and everyday life.

UNESCO describes Lamu as the oldest and best-preserved Swahili settlement in East Africa. The town’s heritage is not limited to its architecture: it is also reflected in its religious festivals, language, food, crafts and community traditions.

Visitors should explore respectfully, dress appropriately in local settings, support local guides and artisans, and ask before photographing residents. Heritage tourism is most valuable when it benefits the people who keep the culture alive.

The Campus View · Opinion

Digital learning is only useful when every learner can access it

By The Campus & Country Desk · 24 November 2026

Digital learning is often presented as the future of education. Devices, online libraries and virtual classrooms can widen access to knowledge, but technology alone cannot guarantee better learning.

A student without reliable internet, electricity, a suitable device or the skills to use digital tools may be left further behind. Institutions must therefore pair technology investment with affordable connectivity, digital literacy training, accessible content and support for learners with disabilities.

Universities and schools should also teach students to question what they find online. Misinformation, plagiarism and unsafe data practices can undermine the benefits of digital platforms. The goal should not simply be to put lessons on screens; it should be to help learners think critically and participate confidently.

Our position: Measure digital education by learning outcomes and equal access—not by the number of devices distributed.
